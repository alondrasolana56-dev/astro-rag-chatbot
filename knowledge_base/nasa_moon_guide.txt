
NASA Artemis - The Next Frontier (2026 Space Apps Focus)
Artemis Program is NASA's mission to return humans to the Moon and establish a permanent base camp at the Lunar South Pole by 2026-2028.

Where is water on the Moon?
Water ice is primarily located at the Lunar South Pole in Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs). NASA's LRO (Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter) and LCROSS mission confirmed water ice in craters like Shackleton Crater, Cabeus Crater, and Faustini Crater. The water is in the form of ice mixed with regolith, estimated at 600 million metric tons. Artemis III will land at the South Pole to drill and extract this water for drinking, oxygen, and rocket fuel (hydrogen/oxygen).

Lunar Challenges for Artemis:
- Extreme temperature: -173C to 127C, requires thermal protection
- LRO (Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter) elevation maps show rugged terrain, craters, boulders - landing hazard
- Radiation exposure, no atmosphere
- Lunar dust (regolith) is abrasive and damages equipment
- Low gravity (1/6 Earth) affects human health and equipment
- Communication delay and blackout in shadowed regions
- Power generation: Need nuclear or solar in areas with limited sunlight

Mars Missions - NASA Focus:
Mars 2020 Perseverance Rover is searching for ancient life in Jezero Crater. Ingenuity Helicopter was first aircraft on Mars. Future: Mars Sample Return mission to bring rocks to Earth, and human mission to Mars in 2030s using Moon as testbed. Challenges: 7-month journey, radiation, thin CO2 atmosphere, dust storms, no water easily accessible. SpaceX Starship and NASA SLS will be used.
